The options, in detail

Camp + Hotel — the recommended plan ~$2,200

Two cars (the Pathfinder + one rental SUV, picked up at San Diego airport so Christmas Day hours aren't an issue). Night one: Jumbo Rocks Campground in Joshua Tree, under a moon that's 99% full. Night two: a real hotel room in Death Valley — Stovepipe Wells or The Ranch, whichever has space. Drives direct between parks (no Route 66 detour) to keep the schedule relaxed.

Death Valley Only ~$735–1,100

Skip Joshua Tree entirely. Drive straight to Death Valley (about 6 hours), camp both nights at Furnace Creek Campground — a single site fits all 7 of you. This is the cheapest way to get a full, unhurried two-park-quality experience out of just one park, and arguably has the single best moment of any option: sandboarding down the dunes at sunset, then walking back out under an almost-full moon.

Highlights

  • Day 1: arrive early afternoon, Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes at sunset, moonlit dune walk that evening
  • Day 2: Badwater Basin, Devil's Golf Course, Artists Drive, Twenty Mule Team Canyon, then an afternoon trip to Rhyolite ghost town (just over the Nevada line) — turn the day into a "find Tatooine" photo hunt using the real Star Wars filming spots
  • Day 3: pack up, drive home (about 6 hours)

Joshua Tree + Mojave ~$900–1,100

Same Joshua Tree day as the recommended plan, but instead of pushing on to Death Valley, spend day two exploring the Route 66/Mojave corridor — Amboy Crater, Roy's classic roadside motel and cafe, and Kelso Dunes (which genuinely "sing" under the right conditions) — then turn back. Least total driving of any two-stop option, and still delivers a real sense of desert adventure without the long Death Valley legs.

Full itinerary mirrors the recommended plan's Day 1, then a relaxed out-and-back along the corridor on Day 2. Ask if you want this built out to the same level of detail.

Grand Road Trip ~$1,450

Both parks, both nights in real beds — a budget motel near Joshua Tree, then Stovepipe Wells in Death Valley. Two cars (Pathfinder + rental SUV). No camping gear needed at all, which removes the biggest cost unknown from the whole comparison. Trades the moonlit-camping memory for simplicity and comfort.

Glamping Hybrid ~$2,000–2,400 est.

AutoCamp Joshua Tree (Airstream suites, pool, a genuinely fun place for kids) for night one, then a nicer Death Valley hotel for night two. The "wow" option — but neither property's exact price for these dates could be confirmed without a phone call, so treat the cost as a rough estimate until you actually call AutoCamp at (844) 366-9715.

RV + Support Car ~$3,000–3,400

A real motorhome (found: a 2018 Forest River Sunseeker in Escondido, sleeps 8, $225/night) plus the Pathfinder as backup, since the RV can't fit down Death Valley's best scenic drive (Artists Drive bans anything over 25ft). This is the "we lived in a motorhome in the desert" experience — genuinely memorable — but turned out considerably more expensive once actual gas costs were added in (an RV gets about 8–9 miles per gallon), and the rental's Sunday return deadline (3pm in Escondido) means the last day is pure driving with no slack for a lie-in or a last Death Valley stop.

If this one appeals despite the cost, message the RV owner first to ask whether a later Sunday return is possible — that alone would make the last day far more relaxed.
Pathfinder Only, No Rental ~$1,390

Just the friends' own car, nothing rented at all — both parks, motel nights. The cheapest way to do both parks properly. The real trade-off: the Pathfinder only has about 16.6 cubic feet of luggage space with all 7 seats in use, so packing needs to be genuinely light, or add a roof box.

Good to know before booking anything

The moon is 99% full on the 25th. Not ideal for stargazing the Milky Way, but it makes a night walk through Joshua Tree's rocks or Death Valley's dunes something special — lean into it rather than fight it.
Shop for groceries and firewood by Dec 24, not Dec 25. The nearest real supermarket (Yucca Valley/Twentynine Palms) is closed Christmas Day, and Joshua Tree doesn't sell firewood inside the park at all — buy it in town first. Death Valley's Furnace Creek store does sell firewood and groceries on-site.
If renting camping gear, pick it up by Dec 24 too — REI closes Christmas Day, same as most rental-car branches and RV depots. One Christmas Eve errand run can cover gear, groceries, and firewood together.
Pick up any rental car at San Diego airport, not a neighborhood branch — airport desks run 24/7, local branches likely don't on Christmas Day.
Daylight is short: sunrise ~6:50am, sunset ~4:40pm all three days. Every itinerary above is built around that — the boring highway driving is deliberately scheduled for early morning or after dark.
