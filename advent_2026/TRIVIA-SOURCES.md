# Trivia sources

The short notes in `src/data/questions.ts` were checked against these museum, cultural institution, government, scientific and specialist sources on 8 November 2026. Legends and traditions are labelled as such; the prana note describes yoga philosophy, not a medical claim. Day 1 retains the wording the user liked.

## Day 1: Gehry

The Guggenheim Museum opened in Bilbao in 1997. Its shimmering titanium curves catch the changing light.

Sources: [Guggenheim Foundation; Guggenheim Museum Bilbao (1)](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/the-guggenheim-museums-and-art-of-this-century), [Guggenheim Foundation; Guggenheim Museum Bilbao (2)](https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/the-building/the-construction).

## Day 2: Malevich

When Black Square was first exhibited in 1915, Malevich hung it high across a corner of the room—the place traditionally reserved for a religious icon in a Russian home.

Sources: [Guggenheim Foundation](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/kazimir-malevich-suprematism-2).

## Day 3: Athena

Athena does not always arrive looking like a goddess. In the Odyssey, she borrows the appearance of Mentor, an old family friend, to guide Odysseus’s son Telemachus.

Sources: [Harvard Center for Hellenic Studies](https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/stamatia-dova-kind-like-a-father-on-mentors-and-kings-in-the-odyssey/).

## Day 4: Jupiter

Jupiter spins once in about ten hours, giving it the shortest day of any planet in our Solar System. Even its largest moon, Ganymede, is bigger than the planet Mercury.

Sources: [NASA (1)](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-facts/), [NASA (2)](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-jupiter-grades-k-4/).

## Day 5: Vivaldi

Vivaldi’s Four Seasons came with poems describing the scenes in the music. In Spring, the viola even plays the part of a barking dog beside a sleeping shepherd.

Sources: [Los Angeles Philharmonic](https://www.laphil.com/works/the-four-seasons).

## Day 6: Nicholas

In paintings, Saint Nicholas often carries three golden balls. They represent the bags of gold he secretly gave to three young women in a famous legend about his generosity.

Sources: [National Gallery, London](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/learn-about-art/paintings-in-depth/painting-saints/recognising-saints-objects/recognising-saints-golden-balls).

## Day 7: Leningrad / The Irony of Fate

Nadya’s “Leningrad” apartment building in The Irony of Fate was actually filmed in Moscow. Her home and Zhenya’s were on the same avenue, at numbers 113 and 125.

Sources: [Moscow city government publication, MN 3/2018](https://www.mos.ru/upload/documents/files/585/MN_3_2018.pdf).

## Day 8: Klimt

The Kiss does not shimmer with gold alone: Klimt also used silver and platinum leaf. Its precious surface changes as the light moves across it.

Sources: [Belvedere collection catalogue](https://sammlung.belvedere.at/objects/6678/der-kuss-liebespaar).

## Day 9: Prana

Yoga traditions describe prana as moving through five vayus, or “winds”. Each has a different role, from drawing energy inward to carrying it throughout the body.

Sources: [Himalayan Institute](https://himalayaninstitute.org/online/series/the-5-vayus/).

## Day 10: Gherkin

Despite the Gherkin’s rounded shape, its exterior glass panels are flat. Just one curved piece crowns the very top: a lens-shaped pane 2.4 metres across.

Sources: [Skanska, construction contractor](https://www.skanska.com/uk/en-gb/news-insights/press-releases/2003/glass-lens-completes-dome-of-30-st-mary-axe).

## Day 11: Slovenia / Lake Bled

Bled Island’s church is reached by 99 stone steps. A local wedding tradition asks the groom to carry his bride up every one of them before they marry there.

Sources: [Bled official tourism board](https://www.bled.si/en/what-to-see-do/attractions/2/bled-island/).

## Day 12: Venice

Venice is spread across 118 small islands. Its canals are the spaces between them, with bridges stitching the city together over the lagoon.

Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/394).

## Day 13: Kahlo

Frida Kahlo had a mirror fitted beneath the canopy of her bed, allowing her to paint self-portraits while lying down. That bed can still be seen in her Blue House museum.

Sources: [Museo Frida Kahlo](https://www.museofridakahlo.org.mx/museo/?lang=en).

## Day 14: Victoria

Victoria and Albert’s family exchanged presents on Christmas Eve, following German tradition. The royal family still follows that custom today.

Sources: [Royal Collection Trust](https://www.rct.uk/collection/stories/a-royal-christmas).

## Day 15: Wright

A glass hatch in Fallingwater’s living room opens onto a staircase that descends towards Bear Run, the stream beneath the house.

Sources: [US National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/restoration-and-replication-of-steel-elements-at-frank-lloyd-wright-s-fallingwater-and-solomon-r-guggenheim-museum.htm).

## Day 16: Paddington

Paddington began with a real toy bear Michael Bond spotted alone on a Selfridges shelf on Christmas Eve in 1956. Bond bought it for his wife, and a story soon followed.

Sources: [Official Paddington musical history](https://london.paddingtonthemusical.com/history-of-paddington/).

## Day 17: Cherry / hanami

Hanami was not always about cherry blossom. Early flower-viewing celebrations in Japan admired plum blossom; cherries became the best-known stars of the tradition later.

Sources: [Japan National Tourism Organization](https://www.japan.travel/en/japans-local-treasures/plum-blossoms-kanazawa-2022/).

## Day 18: Aurora

The northern lights get their colours from different gases: oxygen glows green or red, while nitrogen can glow blue or purple. Even the height of the glowing gas changes the colours you see.

Sources: [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/aurora/en/).

## Day 19: Austen

Austen first called Pride and Prejudice First Impressions. She began that early version around 1796, years before Elizabeth Bennet and Mr Darcy finally reached readers in 1813.

Sources: [Jane Austen’s House (1)](https://janeaustens.house/online-exhibition/the-making-of-pride-and-prejudice/the-making-of-pride-and-prejudice-2/), [Jane Austen’s House (2)](https://janeaustens.house/online-exhibition/the-making-of-pride-and-prejudice/the-making-of-pride-and-prejudice-3/).

## Day 20: Inca

The Incas fitted the finest stone walls at Machu Picchu together without mortar. Each block was shaped to meet its neighbours, like pieces of an enormous stone puzzle.

Sources: [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/machu-picchu-secrets).

## Day 21: Scotland

Christmas Day only became a public holiday in Scotland in 1958. At Hogmanay, a first-footer may bring shortbread or coal, wishing the household food and warmth for the year ahead.

Sources: [Historic Environment Scotland; VisitScotland (1)](https://blog.historicenvironment.scot/2016/12/timeline-christmas-scotland/), [VisitScotland](https://www.visitscotland.com/things-to-do/events/christmas-winter-festivals/hogmanay), [National Coal Mining Museum](https://www.ncm.org.uk/news/what-is-first-footing/).

## Day 22: Webber / Cats

Cats grew from T. S. Eliot’s poems rather than a conventional script. Grizabella, the cat who sings Memory, came from an unpublished poem supplied by Eliot’s widow.

Sources: [Official Cats production history](https://www.catsthemusical.com/origins?9481a8af_page=1), [Smithsonian costume collection](https://www.si.edu/object/dress-grizabella-costume-used-musical-cats%3Anmah_1191024).

## Day 23: Carter

Before finding Tutankhamun, Carter went to Egypt at just seventeen to work as an archaeological artist. His careful watercolours recorded ancient paintings as well as the birds and wildlife around him.

Sources: [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/museum-life/before-tutankhamun-howard-carters-watercolours).

## Day 24: Nutcracker

The Nutcracker premiered in 1892. The Sugar Plum Fairy’s bell-like celesta looks a little like a piano, but its felt hammers strike metal plates rather than strings.

Sources: [Birmingham Royal Ballet](https://www.brb.org.uk/stories/heavenly-tunes-of-the-nutcracker).
