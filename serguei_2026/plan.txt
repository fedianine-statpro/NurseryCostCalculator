Build a small, polished, self-contained browser game as a 70th birthday present for Sergey.

The game should take approximately 8 to 10 minutes to complete.

It will be hosted on GitHub Pages, so it must be fully static and client-side only. Do not use a backend, database, authentication, external API, build server, or anything that requires server-side execution.

Use plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript unless there is a very strong reason to do otherwise. Keep the project easy to understand and edit manually later.

## 1. Core concept

The game is an interactive special anniversary issue of a fictional family magazine:

**СЕРГЕЙ**
**Юбилейный спецвыпуск**
**Независимое семейное издание, основано в детстве**

The framing is based on a real fact: as a child, Sergey made homemade magazines for his family using newspaper clippings, his own drawings and articles.

The conceit of the game is that this childhood editorial office somehow continued operating for the next 60+ years and has now assembled an official archival biography of its founder.

The editorial office is:
- very serious;
- slightly pompous;
- highly bureaucratic;
- convinced that it is conducting rigorous historical research;
- occasionally contradicted by family evidence;
- capable of drawing absurd conclusions from perfectly real events;
- affectionate rather than mocking.

The player is Sergey himself.

The game should feel like he is opening an archive about his own life and occasionally helping the editorial office verify, classify or interpret events.

The comedy should emerge from the system and presentation rather than from random one-line jokes.

The humour should use:
- mock-serious journalism;
- archival investigation;
- conflicting testimony;
- absurd bureaucratic classifications;
- disproportionate celebration of mundane successes;
- overly rigorous statistics about things that should not be quantified;
- callbacks;
- gradual escalation;
- the contrast between official history and family memory.

Do not make the interface itself confusing or deceptive.

## 2. Critical UX requirement

The player is 70 years old and may not be familiar with games.

At no point should Sergey think that:
- he broke the game;
- the browser malfunctioned;
- he clicked the wrong thing;
- he is stuck;
- he failed;
- he needs gaming knowledge.

Therefore:

- One main action per screen.
- Maximum 3 large choices at once.
- Large readable buttons.
- Large text.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- No hidden buttons.
- No pixel hunting.
- No drag and drop.
- No twitch mechanics.
- No timers requiring fast reactions.
- No keyboard combinations.
- No fake crashes.
- No disappearing UI.
- No buttons that move away from the cursor.
- No misleading navigation.
- No dead ends.
- No "Game Over".
- Any choice should allow the story to continue.
- If a humorous answer is "wrong", show a funny short consequence and then naturally continue or offer an obvious "Вернуться к фактам" button.
- Always provide a clearly visible way to proceed.

The humour should happen after a clear action, never instead of a clear action.

## 3. Tone

The game should be funny, warm, intelligent and personal.

Avoid generic birthday-card humour such as:
- "Level 70 unlocked"
- "Old but gold"
- jokes about being ancient
- jokes about poor memory
- jokes about ageing bodies
- "boomer" humour
- excessive jokes about alcohol
- random memes

The humour should be specific to Sergey's life.

The editorial narrator should sound like a combination of:
- Soviet newspaper seriousness;
- historical archive;
- corporate audit;
- family historian;
- documentary narrator who occasionally discovers that the evidence is inconvenient.

The joke construction should often follow:

setup -> establish rule -> repeat rule -> expectation -> contradiction -> payoff.

Callbacks are important.

## 4. Visual identity

The visual language should combine:

1. Handmade childhood family magazine
2. Old newspaper clippings
3. Archival folders and stamps
4. Period-appropriate visual changes as the decades progress
5. Family scrapbook
6. Clean modern interaction controls

The content itself can look tactile and historical, but navigation must remain modern and obvious.

Use:
- paper textures created with CSS where possible;
- monochrome or slightly aged newspaper-style panels;
- clipping edges;
- typed headings;
- handwritten-looking annotations sparingly;
- stamps such as "ПРОВЕРЕНО", "ОСПОРЕНО", "АРХИВ", "СЕКРЕТНО";
- fake classification marks;
- dates and issue numbers;
- old-style newspaper columns for short extracts;
- clipping cards layered on a desk or archive background.

Do not make long text look like a dense newspaper. Readability is more important than visual authenticity.

For actual personal photographs, create clear placeholder image paths and captions so they can later be replaced easily.

Suggested structure:

assets/
  photos/
  audio/
  illustrations/
  newspaper/
  icons/

Use obvious filenames such as:
- sergey-childhood.jpg
- sergey-army.jpg
- sergey-irina.jpg
- cuba-family.jpg
- canada-house.jpg
- sergey-fishing.jpg

If an asset is missing, display a tasteful placeholder rather than a broken image.

## 5. Main game structure

The game should be one continuous story divided into approximately 8 chapters.

Target total playtime: 8 to 10 minutes.

Do not create twenty disconnected mini-games. Reuse the same visual language and editorial office throughout.

### INTRO

Black or dark archival opening.

Text:

"Редакция семейного журнала представляет"

Then reveal:

"СЕРГЕЙ"
"Юбилейный спецвыпуск"

Small text:

"Материалы собирались с 1955 года."

Then:

"Перед публикацией редакция просит главного героя проверить некоторые спорные факты."

Button:

"Открыть архив"

Then show a mock archive report:

"Обнаружено: 70 лет материалов"
"Фотографии: имеются"
"Свидетельства родственников: имеются"
"Противоречия в свидетельствах: имеются"
"Редакционная уверенность: необоснованно высокая"

Button:
"Начать проверку"

## 6. Chapter 1: Dushanbe

Year: 1955 onward.

Facts:
- Sergey was born in Dushanbe in 1955.
- His parents were Mira and Yura.
- As a child he became interested in hockey, probably field hockey.
- He made magazines for the family from newspaper clippings, drawings and his own articles.
- He liked chess.

This chapter establishes the magazine itself.

Show an old homemade magazine style page.

Editorial credits:

"Главный редактор: Сергей"
"Автор статей: Сергей"
"Художник: Сергей"
"Отдел газетных вырезок: Сергей"
"Распространение: среди родственников"
"Отдел жалоб читателей: отсутствует"

Then introduce three childhood interests as clickable archive pieces:
- hockey stick / hockey clipping;
- chess knight;
- homemade magazine.

Player can inspect them in any order.

Keep each interaction under 10 seconds.

Then the editorial office performs a "career prediction":

"На основании ранних интересов редакция может с высокой точностью определить будущую профессию Сергея."

Choices:
- Хоккеист
- Гроссмейстер
- Медиамагнат

Whatever the player chooses, perform a mock calculation and reveal:

"БУХГАЛТЕР"

Pause.

"Редакционная модель прогнозирования временно снята с эксплуатации."

This should be one of the first strong jokes.

## 7. Chapter 2: Moscow and university

The family moves from Dushanbe to Moscow.

Show this as the editorial archive receiving a change-of-address notice.

Then university.

Sergey studies accounting and also attends a military department.

Present this as a bureaucratic form:

"СПЕЦИАЛЬНОСТЬ: БУХГАЛТЕР"
button/checkmark: "Подтвердить"

Then:
"ВОЕННАЯ КАФЕДРА: ПРОЙДЕНА"
button/checkmark

Then a large official stamp:

"ПОЗДРАВЛЯЕМ.
ВЫ ЛЕЙТЕНАНТ."

Editorial note:

"Советская система профессиональной ориентации установила, что человек, умеющий сводить баланс, также способен командовать людьми."

Do not imply that this is historically official. It is clearly fictional editorial humour.

## 8. Chapter 3: Army archive

Important facts:
- Sergey served as a lieutenant.
- He became interested in boxing.
- He learned guitar.
- Vladimir Vysotsky became a lifelong idol.
- Sergey sometimes allowed ordinary soldiers to sleep in his lieutenant accommodation or space so they could avoid dedovshchina / bullying.
- As an officer, Sergey himself was not being bullied.
- There was an incident where Sergey, while drunk, picked flowers and was detained.

Do NOT frame boxing as Sergey being beaten or being vulnerable to hazing.

The army section should show four archive folders:

"БОКС"
"ГИТАРА"
"ВЫСОЦКИЙ"
"ДЕЛО О ЦВЕТАХ"

### Boxing clipping

Use a newspaper clipping:

"СПОРТИВНАЯ ХРОНИКА"

"Лейтенант Сергей начал заниматься боксом."

Then:

"Причины, по которым бухгалтеру потребовался поставленный удар, архив не уточняет."

Possible alternate line:
"Редакция подтверждает: данный метод впоследствии не применялся при аудите."

Use only one of these, not both, to avoid over-writing the joke.

### Helping soldiers

This should be warm and revealing rather than flippant.

Possible mock article:

"МОЛОДОЙ ЛЕЙТЕНАНТ ПОДОЗРЕВАЕТСЯ В НЕУСТАВНОЙ ДОБРОТЕ"

"По сведениям редакции, некоторые солдаты получали возможность спать у Сергея, чтобы избежать дедовщины."

"Военное ведомство не подтвердило существование официальной категории 'нормально выспаться'."

This fact should later become a subtle callback showing Sergey's character.

### Guitar and Vysotsky

Show guitar and Vysotsky as separate small archive items.

For Vysotsky:

"ИНТЕРЕС ОБНАРУЖЕН"
"Статус: сохранён навсегда"

This status may later appear again in Canada and at the end.

### Flowers incident

Treat this as a classified criminal-history parody only if presented affectionately.

Header:

"ДЕЛО № ███"
"Повреждение городского цветочного фонда"

Body:
"Обстоятельства происшествия редакция считает достаточно хорошо известными семье и недостаточно выгодными для дальнейшего уточнения."

Button:
"Закрыть дело"

After click:

"Дело закрыто."

Then, after a short pause:

"До следующего семейного праздника."

Do not over-focus on alcohol.

Sergey meets his future wife Irina.

Immediately stamp the clipping:

"ОСПОРЕНО ИРИНОЙ"

Then revise the report:

Pause.

"Дополнительное уточнение: сильно опоздал."

Then:

"Редакция проверяет, каким образом существуют последующие главы."

Large button:
"Попытаться спасти сюжет"

On click, dramatic celebration:

"ИРИНА ОСТАЛАСЬ"

"КРИТИЧЕСКАЯ СЮЖЕТНАЯ ЛИНИЯ СОХРАНЕНА"

Then briefly show:

"После армии Сергей и Ирина поженились."

Important recurring device:

Introduce source reliability labels:

"Источник: Сергей"
"Надёжность: высокая"

## 12. Chapter 7: Canada, 1999

This should shift tone for approximately 30 to 45 seconds.

Facts:
- In 1999 Sergey moved with his family to Canada.
- He had difficulty finding work.
- The family had very little money.
- He eventually found work.
- He worked at companies including Bayer, Oracle and Shell.
- His career was connected to SAP.
- The family sold their Moscow apartment very cheaply and later bought a house in Canada.

Do not make the difficult migration period into slapstick.

Use a minimalist interaction.

Screen:

"1999."
"Новая страна."
"Работы нет."
"Денег почти нет."
"Семья здесь."

One large button:

"Продолжать"

Click.

Show a rejection or empty job result.

Same button again:

"Продолжать"

Click.

Another setback.

Third time:

"Продолжать"

Then gradually reveal:

"BAYER"
"ORACLE"
"SHELL"

Editorial note:

"В этой части архива обнаружена необычная закономерность."

"Сергей продолжал нажимать 'Продолжать' значительно дольше, чем система предполагала."

This should be warm and understated.

Then show a callback to the army chapter:

"Архивная справка:"
"Сергей уже однажды использовал своё положение, чтобы кому-то стало легче."
"В Канаде положения пока не было."

### Newspaper clippings

## 17. Interaction model

Create a reusable scene engine.

Each scene should be described in data rather than hard-coded into different pages where practical.

A scene may contain:
- year/date;
- section title;
- headline;
- body text;
- image;
- clipping;
- 1 to 3 choices;
- short reaction;
- transition;
- optional callback;
- optional sound.

Prefer a JSON-like JavaScript data structure such as:

const story = [
  {
    id: "dushanbe-career",
    year: "1960-е",
    type: "choice",
    ...
  }
];

Build reusable rendering functions for:
- article scene;
- choice scene;
- archive folder;
- photo scene;
- official document;
- stamp animation;
- reveal;
- transition;
- final montage.

This is important because the family will likely revise text later.

## 18. Content configuration

Put all editable personal text and facts in a dedicated file if practical:

story-data.js

or:

data/story.js

Do not scatter biography text throughout UI code.

Create clear comments showing:
- where to edit text;
- where to replace photos;
- where to change dates;
- where to enable/disable scenes.

## 19. Save/progress

The game is only 8 to 10 minutes, but accidental refresh should not be catastrophic.

Use localStorage only for:
- current chapter/scene;
- whether the intro has already been seen.

On load, if progress exists, offer:

"Продолжить с места остановки"
"Начать сначала"

Do not silently resume without asking.

Add a small discreet "Начать сначала" option in the menu.

## 20. Audio

Audio is optional but useful.

Do not autoplay sound before user interaction.

After the first click, audio may be enabled.

Provide a clearly visible sound toggle.

Possible sound types:
- paper rustle;
- stamp;
- typewriter;
- subtle newspaper press sound;
- short fanfare for the Irina scene;
- soft ambient transitions.

Do not use copyrighted Vysotsky recordings unless the family provides audio and has the right to use it.

Instead create a placeholder:
assets/audio/vysotsky-placeholder.mp3

Do not ship copyrighted music by default.

## 21. Accessibility

Design for an older player.

Minimum preferred body text on desktop: approximately 20px.

Buttons: large touch/click targets.

Strong contrast.

Avoid light grey text.

Avoid very thin fonts.

Avoid all-caps for long sentences.

Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

Animations should be short and skippable by clicking "Дальше".

Do not make understanding dependent on animation.

Support keyboard Enter/Space for focused buttons, but mouse/touch must remain the primary interaction.

## 22. Responsive layout

Primary target:
- desktop/laptop browser;
- tablet should also work.

Minimum usable width: 320px.

Do not create horizontal page scrolling.

Newspaper clippings can stack vertically on smaller screens.

Keep the main interactive area centred and not excessively wide.

## 23. Technical requirements

Opening index.html locally should work wherever browser security permits.

GitHub Pages deployment should require only pushing files.

No external fonts required. If using web fonts, provide graceful fallback and do not make the game depend on them.

## 26. Quality bar for humour

Before finalizing each joke, ask:

1. Does it depend on something specific about Sergey?
2. Does it grow naturally out of the editorial/archive premise?
3. Is there a setup and payoff?
4. Is it affectionate?
5. Does it avoid explaining the joke?
6. Would it still be funny without adding an emoji or meme phrase?
7. Does it preserve the dignity of the real event?

If the answer to several of these is no, rewrite it.

Avoid inserting random jokes simply to make every screen funny.

Some scenes, especially the Canada job-search section and the final family message, should be allowed to breathe.

## 28. Final result

The finished game should feel like:

"Sergey once made a magazine for his family. Seventy years later, his family has made the ultimate issue of that magazine about him."

It should begin as a funny archival investigation, grow into a portrait of his character, and end as a sincere family present.

The player should finish smiling rather than feeling that he completed a quiz.

The game is not about testing how well Sergey remembers his life.

It is about letting him rediscover his life through an affectionate, slightly unreliable family archive.
